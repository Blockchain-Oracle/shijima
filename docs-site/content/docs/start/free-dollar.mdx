To try Shijima without buying anything, some wallets can claim **$1 of USDG plus 0.00008 ETH** (about 30¢) for network fees. That ETH covers creating an agent, putting the $1 in, and taking it out later.

<Callout type="warn" title="It runs out">
There is no fixed number. The card counts how many gifts the gift wallet can still pay, for example **2 left**. When it can't pay another, it says **All the free dollars are gone.**
</Callout>

## Who can claim

- You must be signed in. The $1 goes to the wallet you signed in with.
- One claim per wallet.
- One claim per internet connection, ever. We keep a salted hash of the connection's address, not the address itself.

## How to claim

Find the card **Free $1 to try** on your Wallet page, in step 2 of creating an agent, or in the sidebar. Slide **Slide to claim your $1**.

It says **Claimed. It will arrive in your wallet in about a minute.** Then **Sent: $1 and a little ETH for fees are in your wallet**, with links to both transfers on the explorer.

## Is $1 enough to do anything?

Yes. Your agent's smallest trade is **$0.20**, and Shijima pays the network fee for every trade your agent makes. So $1 is enough for a real, live trade you can check on the chain.

## Behind the card

The website only writes your claim into a queue. The worker holds the gift wallet's key and sends the USDG first, then the ETH. Each transfer is saved before it is broadcast, so a retry after a restart never pays twice.
