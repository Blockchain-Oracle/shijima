## Standing rules

A standing rule is a safety line you draw in advance. It reads as one sentence:

> If **Nvidia** falls more than **5%** below its reference, sell **50%** of it.

- The fall can be **1% to 20%**. The sale can be **10% to all** of the holding.
- You can have up to **10** rules.
- The **reference** is the same reference price every decision is measured against ([how it decides](/agent/how-it-decides#the-reference-price)). The fall is measured on the pool's 30-minute average price, so one odd trade cannot set it off.

Add them in step 3 of creating your agent, under **More options**, or later under **Edit what you told it** → **Rules**, or by asking in the chat. Press **Add a rule** or **Remove this rule**.

<Example>
You hold **$200** of Nvidia and have the rule above. On Sunday Nvidia's pool drifts **6%** below Friday's reference. Your rule fires: your agent wants to sell half, about **$94**. If your per-trade limit is $10, it sells $10 now, and the rule is still there on the next check.
</Example>

### What a rule does and does not do

- A rule raises a sale **before** any drift trade, and it takes the place of any drift trade in the same stock.
- The sale is **protective**, so two checks that would normally stop a trade are relaxed: the gap to the reference and the cost of trading. That is the point of a rule: it acts when the price has moved.
- It still goes through your limits, the contract's 8% price floor and your mode. In Practice it only writes down what it would have sold; in Ask me first it asks you.
- The timing question is still asked, and the record names the rule that raised the sale.

## Notes for your agent

**Notes for your agent** is free text, up to 600 characters when you create it. For example: “I care more about keeping Nvidia than about the exact weights.” Your agent reads your notes when it decides the timing of a move. Notes never change what you hold, the size of a trade or a limit.

Your notes stay private. The public record only shows that a note applied, never its text.
